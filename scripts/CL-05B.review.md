## Judgment calls — the antisemitism
- The video names the play's antisemitism directly in the context scene and the close, and frames
  Shylock as both a product of a hateful age and the humanised, tragic centre the text keeps producing
  against its comic frame — the standard modern critical position. It does not stage Shylock as a
  simple villain, nor whitewash the forced conversion (presented as the destruction of a person).
- Structured in five movements; Portia foregrounded as the play's real intelligence/power.
- Art deliberately uses Rembrandt's dignified Jewish portraits for Shylock rather than caricature.
