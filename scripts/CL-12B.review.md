## Plot accuracy (keyed to the novella's chapters)
- "Story of the Door": Utterson & Enfield; the door; Hyde tramples the child; the cheque signed by a
  respectable man. ✓
- "Search for Mr Hyde": Jekyll's will leaves all to Hyde; Utterson suspects blackmail; he meets Hyde
  (the wave of loathing); Jekyll evasive, insists Hyde's rights be protected. ✓
- "The Carew Murder Case": Hyde clubs Sir Danvers Carew to death; the broken cane is Jekyll's; manhunt;
  Hyde vanishes; Jekyll produces a letter and reforms. ✓
- "Incident of the Letter / Remarkable Incident of Dr Lanyon": the forged letter; Jekyll relapses into
  seclusion; Lanyon dies of shock, leaving a sealed account. ✓
- "The Last Night": Poole fetches Utterson; the voice in the lab isn't Jekyll's; they axe the door and
  find Hyde dead by poison in Jekyll's clothes; Jekyll gone; two documents left. ✓
- "Lanyon's Narrative": Hyde transforms into Jekyll before Lanyon's eyes; the shock kills him. ✓
- "Henry Jekyll's Full Statement": the double life; the theory of the two selves; the potion; becoming
  Hyde (smaller, younger, pure evil); the double life of pleasure then cruelty; involuntary
  transformations; the Carew murder; the park relapse; the failed salt; the final confession and suicide. ✓

## Judgment calls
- Told as the original mystery, then the confession — preserving the structure Stevenson used; flagged
  up front that modern readers know the twist.
- Hyde's "pure evil" / "deformity" follow the text; no physical description invented (as Stevenson
  withholds one deliberately).
- Context framing (Deacon Brodie, Edinburgh's double city, the pre-Freudian unconscious, the addiction
  reading) is standard, widely documented background.
- Title shortened on the title-card for legibility; full title in meta. End-screen points to CL-01B.
