## Plot accuracy notes
- Plague on Thebes; Oedipus the revered king who earlier solved the Sphinx's riddle and won the throne + the widowed queen Jocasta; sends Creon to the Delphic oracle; answer = Thebes harbours Laius's unpunished murderer, expel him to lift the plague: correct.
- Oedipus's public curse on the killer (extending it to himself if the killer is under his roof): correct — dramatic irony.
- Tiresias (blind seer) summoned, refuses, then goaded declares Oedipus is the murderer/pollution and foretells his blindness & exile; Oedipus's temper; accuses Creon of a plot to seize the throne; Jocasta intervenes: correct.
- Jocasta dismisses oracles: Laius prophesied to die by his own son, but their baby was exposed (feet pinned) and Laius (she says) killed by robbers at a place where three roads meet; the "three roads" detail alarms Oedipus: correct.
- Oedipus's story: raised prince of Corinth (Polybus & Merope); a drunk's taunt; Delphic oracle — fated to kill his father & marry his mother; fled Corinth; killed an old man & party at a crossroads; sends for the surviving shepherd/witness (hope hinges on "robbers" plural vs one man): correct.
- Corinthian messenger: Polybus dead of old age (Jocasta: prophecy void); but Oedipus fears his mother lives; messenger reveals Oedipus was adopted — he received the pin-footed baby on Cithaeron from a shepherd of Laius's house: correct. The Sphinx riddle (four/two/three legs = Man) and "Oedipus = swollen foot": correct.
- Jocasta realises the truth first, begs him to stop, he misreads as snobbery about low birth, she flees: correct.
- The shepherd, under threat, confirms: the baby (Laius & Jocasta's son, prophesied to kill his father, feet pierced) was spared out of pity and given to Corinth — i.e., Oedipus; the full truth lands (killed his father Laius, married his mother Jocasta): correct.
- Offstage (reported by messenger): Jocasta hangs herself; Oedipus takes the gold brooch-pins from her gown and blinds himself; staggers out, begs exile to lift the plague; Creon takes power; Oedipus's farewell to his daughters (Antigone & Ismene): correct.
