## Plot accuracy (keyed to the poem)
- Hrothgar builds Heorot; Grendel (kin of Cain) terrorises it nightly for 12 years. ✓
- Beowulf the Geat sails to Denmark; the feast (and Unferth's taunt); vows to fight bare-handed;
  tears off Grendel's arm; Grendel flees to die; the arm hung as a trophy; celebration. ✓
- Grendel's mother attacks in revenge, kills Hrothgar's counsellor; the haunted mere; Beowulf dives;
  the borrowed sword fails; the giant-forged sword; he beheads her and Grendel; blood melts the blade. ✓
- Return, reward, Hrothgar's warning speech on pride and mortality; Beowulf sails home, later king. ✓
- 50 years on: a stolen cup wakes the dragon; it burns the land; old Beowulf fights it, sensing his
  death; his men flee but Wiglaf; together they kill the dragon; Beowulf is mortally poisoned. ✓
- Beowulf sees the gold, names Wiglaf heir, asks for the barrow; dies; Wiglaf rebukes the cowards;
  the Geats' doom foretold; the funeral pyre, the lament, the reburied hoard, the barrow, the riders. ✓

## Judgment calls
- Structured in five movements (Grendel → the Barrow), the poem's own three-fight arc.
- *wyrd*/fate and the pagan-vs-Christian overlay surfaced as themes (standard scholarship).
- Monsters characterised as the poem does (Grendel = kin of Cain/outcast; the mother = avenger; the
  dragon = impersonal greed/destruction).
