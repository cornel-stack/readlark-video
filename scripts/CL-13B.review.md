## Plot accuracy (keyed to the novel)
- Frame: Lockwood rents the Grange, visits Heights, dreams of the ghost-child Catherine; Heathcliff's
  grief; Nelly Dean narrates — Ch. 1–4. ✓
- Old Earnshaw brings Heathcliff from Liverpool; Catherine bonds, Hindley resents — Ch. 4. ✓
- Earnshaw dies; Hindley degrades Heathcliff to servant — Ch. 5–6. ✓
- The Grange visit / dog bite; Catherine civilised — Ch. 6. ✓
- Catherine accepts Edgar; the confession to Nelly ("more myself than I am"; souls the same; "I am
  Heathcliff"); Heathcliff overhears the "degrade" part and flees in the storm — Ch. 9. ✓
- Catherine marries Edgar; three years' calm; Heathcliff returns transformed — Ch. 10. ✓
- Heathcliff marries Isabella out of spite; Catherine's brain-fever and delirium — Ch. 11–12. ✓
- The violent final reunion; Catherine dies birthing young Cathy; Heathcliff begs to be haunted — Ch. 15–16. ✓
- Heathcliff seizes the Heights from the drunken Hindley; degrades Hareton — Ch. 17. ✓
- Second generation (Hareton, young Cathy, sickly Linton); Heathcliff imprisons Cathy and forces her
  marriage to Linton; Edgar and Linton die; he gains the Grange — Ch. 21–28. ✓
- Heathcliff haunted, loses the will to destroy, starves toward death; found dead at the open window,
  smiling; buried beside Catherine with loosened coffins — Ch. 33–34. ✓
- Young Cathy teaches Hareton to read; their love breaks the cycle; the closing graves and last line — Ch. 32–34. ✓

## Judgment calls
- Structured in six movements across the two generations (the usual way to teach the novel), keeping
  the frame (Lockwood/Nelly) explicit so the double narration isn't confusing.
- Heathcliff's origins and his three missing years are left unexplained, exactly as Brontë leaves them.
- "Gipsy/dark-skinned" wording follows the novel's own ambiguous treatment of Heathcliff's race.
- End-screen points to CL-01B (Macbeth retelling), not a 10-min companion (A-format discontinued).
