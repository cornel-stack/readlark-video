## Plot accuracy notes
- Setting: Puritan Boston, 1640s; the 'Custom-House' framing preface (found-manuscript device); Hawthorne's Puritan/Salem-judge ancestry: correct.
- Hester Prynne led from prison with baby Pearl; crime = adultery (husband an older scholar, sent her ahead, presumed lost at sea); sentence = scaffold + wearing the scarlet 'A'; she embroiders it gorgeously; refuses to name the father; sees her husband at the crowd's edge: correct.
- The husband visits her in prison as a "physician", refuses to share her disgrace, swears her to secrecy, takes the alias Roger Chillingworth, vows to find & secretly punish the lover: correct.
- Hester stays (cottage on the town edge), lives by needlework, endures, does years of charity → the 'A' shifts toward "Able" for many: correct.
- Pearl: wild, elfish, dressed crimson/gold = living letter; obsessed with the 'A'; the magistrates try to take her, Dimmesdale intervenes (and she lays her cheek on his hand): correct.
- Arthur Dimmesdale: revered young minister, secretly the father, wasting with guilt, hand at his heart; secret vigils/self-scourging; vague pulpit half-confessions: correct.
- Chillingworth suspects him, moves in as his physician/companion ("leech"), psychological torture; sees the mark on the sleeping minister's chest (ecstasy of triumph): correct.
- The midnight scaffold scene (Dimmesdale, Hester, Pearl; the meteor 'A'): correct.
- Forest meeting: Hester reveals Chillingworth is her husband; they plan to flee to Europe; she throws off the 'A' and lets down her hair; the sunlight; Pearl refuses to come until the letter is replaced: correct.
- Election-day sermon (his greatest/last); Dimmesdale mounts the scaffold, calls Hester & Pearl, confesses, bares a scarlet mark on his own flesh, dies in Hester's arms; Pearl kisses him: correct.
- Chillingworth, revenge gone, withers & dies within a year, leaves his fortune to Pearl (who marries well abroad); Hester leaves then returns years later, voluntarily resumes the 'A', becomes a counsellor to suffering women; buried by Dimmesdale under one tombstone with the letter: correct.

## Judgment calls
- Exact mark on Dimmesdale's chest: Hawthorne is deliberately ambiguous (he never states plainly what Chillingworth saw, nor the nature of Dimmesdale's self-branding); the script preserves that ambiguity ("sees something… enough").
- Theme emphasis — the confessed/open sin (Hester, who heals) vs the hidden/festering sin (Dimmesdale, who rots) and revenge as self-damnation (Chillingworth) — is the standard and well-supported reading; "Be true" is Hawthorne's own stated moral.
- The 'Custom-House' preface is summarized (not dramatized) as the found-manuscript frame — accurate, and avoids bogging the retelling down.
- "scarlet letter" as a phrase that entered the language (public branding for private failing) noted in the close — accurate and apt.
