## Plot accuracy (keyed to the play)
- The tempest/apparent wreck; Miranda & Prospero; the storm was his magic, no one harmed. ✓
- Ferdinand led by Ariel's "Full fathom five"; the passengers scattered. ✓
- Prospero's backstory: Duke of Milan, usurped by Antonio + King Alonso, set adrift with baby Miranda,
  saved by Gonzalo's provisioning; 12 years on the island. ✓
- Ferdinand & Miranda fall in love; Prospero feigns harshness and tests Ferdinand with log-hauling. ✓
- Ariel (freed from Sycorax's pine, serves unwillingly, longs for freedom); Caliban (island native,
  son of Sycorax, enslaved after the assault on Miranda; the colonial reading). ✓
- Caliban/Stephano/Trinculo murder-plot; Antonio tempts Sebastian to kill the sleeping Alonso; Ariel
  foils it. ✓
- The vanishing banquet and harpy ("three men of sin"); the wedding masque; "such stuff as dreams". ✓
- Ariel's pity moves Prospero to mercy ("the rarer action…"); he abjures magic (break staff, drown
  book); reveals himself, forgives Antonio & Alonso, reclaims Milan; Ferdinand revealed (the chess);
  frees Caliban and Ariel; the epilogue asking the audience to set him free. ✓

## Judgment calls
- Structured in five movements; foregrounds the revenge-vs-forgiveness turn and the Shakespeare's-
  farewell reading of Prospero (standard, widely held).
- Caliban handled with care: both his real menace (the attempted assault) and the just grievance /
  post-colonial reading presented, without over-claiming authorial intent.
- Antonio's unrepentant silence at the end flagged as a deliberate dark spot (textual).
