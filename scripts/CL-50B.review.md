## Plot accuracy (keyed to the novel's 12 chapters)
- The Brahmin's Son: gifted, restless, dissatisfied with inherited ritual knowledge. ✓
- With the Samanas: joins the ascetics with Govinda; masters fasting/self-denial; finds it only escape. ✓
- Gotama: they meet the Buddha; Govinda joins the order; Siddhartha declines, arguing enlightenment
  is experience, not transferable doctrine. ✓
- Awakening: resolves to embrace the world and learn from himself; feels reborn and alone. ✓
- Kamala / Amongst the People / Sansara: crosses the river (Vasudeva); Kamala teaches him love; he
  becomes a rich merchant under Kamaswami; is slowly corrupted — greed, gambling, the silenced voice. ✓
- By the River: the dead-songbird dream; abandons everything; near-suicide; the syllable Om saves him;
  Govinda watches over his sleep. ✓
- The Ferryman: lives with Vasudeva; learns to listen to the river; the vision of time/simultaneity. ✓
- The Son: Kamala dies of a snakebite, leaving their son; Siddhartha's helpless love; the boy flees. ✓
- Om / Govinda: full enlightenment at the river; Vasudeva departs into the forest; the closing scene
  with Govinda and the vision of all faces at the kiss. ✓

## Judgment calls
- Structured in five movements (Brahmin's son → Om), compressing the 12 chapters; the Buddha is
  carefully distinguished from the hero Siddhartha (a common point of confusion).
- Hindu/Buddhist terms (Atman, Brahman, Sansara, Om) glossed in plain language.
- Jung/counterculture framing in the context scene is standard, widely documented background.
- End-screen points to CL-01B (no 10-min companion; A-format discontinued).
