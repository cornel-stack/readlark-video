## Plot accuracy notes
- Rome a king-hating Republic; Caesar returned triumphant after civil war, adored, hugely powerful; aristocrats uneasy; the Lupercal — Antony offers the crown thrice, Caesar refuses (increasingly reluctantly); the soothsayer's "Beware the Ides of March": correct.
- Cassius (republican + envious, the Tiber-rescue & "groan like a sick girl" story) recruits Brutus; "fault not in our stars"; needs Brutus's honour as legitimizing figurehead: correct.
- Brutus's soliloquy (no personal grievance; reasons from principle/the future — "kill the serpent in the egg"; "sacrificers not butchers"); joins; overrules Cassius (spare Antony, let Antony speak); wife Portia (self-wound to prove constancy, later suicide by "swallowing fire"): correct.
- The night of portents; Calpurnia's blood-statue dream & plea; Decius reinterprets it + the crown/henpecked-coward goading; "cowards die many times"; the ignored written warning (Artemidorus) on the steps; "the Ides are come / Ay, but not gone": correct.
- The assassination at the Senate (Pompey's statue); Casca strikes first; Caesar fights till he sees Brutus; "Et tu, Brute?"; conspirators bathe hands in blood, cry "Liberty! Freedom!"; the "lofty scene… acted over in states unborn" meta-moment: correct.
- Antony spared, feigns accord, shakes bloody hands, asks to speak; Cassius warns; Brutus overrules (speaks first, conditions); Brutus's reasoned prose speech ("not that I loved Caesar less, but that I loved Rome more") wins the crowd briefly; Antony's oration (ironic "honourable men", the wounds, the mantle, Brutus's "unkindest cut", withholding then revealing the will — money & walks to citizens) turns the mob to riot; conspirators flee: correct.
- Second Triumvirate (Antony, Octavius, Lepidus), proscription death-list scene (bartering lives, cheating Lepidus); Brutus & Cassius raise armies; the tent quarrel & reconciliation; Portia's death; Caesar's ghost ("thy evil spirit… at Philippi"): correct.
- Philippi: Cassius misreads the field (thinks Titinius captured), has his servant Pindarus kill him with the Caesar-sword, on his birthday — then it proves a mistake; Brutus "the last of the Romans"/"Caesar, thou art mighty yet"; Brutus runs on his sword (held by Strato); "I killed not thee with half so good a will"; Antony's epitaph "the noblest Roman of them all… this was a man"; Octavius orders honourable burial: correct.
- Theme: killing Caesar to save the Republic instead destroys it → civil war & the first emperor (Octavius/Augustus); good intentions, catastrophic results; reason vs emotion in the two speeches: correct and central.
