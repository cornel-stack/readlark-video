## Plot accuracy (keyed to the novel)
- Gateshead: orphan Jane, cruel Mrs Reed & cousin John; the red-room terror. ✓
- Lowood: Brocklehurst's cruelty; Miss Temple; Helen Burns; the typhus epidemic and Helen's death;
  Jane stays eight years, then advertises for a governess post. ✓
- Thornfield: governess to Adèle; Mrs Fairfax; the locked third floor and the laugh (blamed on Grace
  Poole); meeting Rochester; the bed-fire she puts out; Blanche Ingram ruse; the garden proposal; the
  split chestnut; the torn veil. ✓
- The wedding stopped; Bertha Mason revealed as Rochester's living mad wife; his account of the
  Caribbean marriage; Rochester begs Jane to be his mistress; she refuses and flees. ✓
- Destitute on the moors; saved by St John Rivers and his sisters; inherits a fortune; discovers they
  are her cousins; shares the money; St John's loveless missionary proposal; the telepathic call;
  she refuses St John and returns to find Thornfield burned. ✓
- Bertha's fire; Rochester blinded and maimed trying to save her; Bertha's death; Ferndean; the
  reunion of equals; "Reader, I married him." ✓

## Judgment calls
- Structured in five movements across the novel's five settings (Gateshead/Lowood, Thornfield, the
  wedding, Moor House, Ferndean).
- Bertha presented both as plot-device and as the novel's debated symbol (the caged first wife / Jane's
  dark double) without over-committing to one modern reading.
- The telepathic "call" kept as the one supernatural beat, as in the text.
