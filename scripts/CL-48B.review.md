## Plot accuracy notes
- Three acts over Christmas (Eve / Christmas Day / the following evening): correct.
- Torvald's bank promotion; his pet names (songbird, squirrel, spendthrift); treating Nora as a child/pet; the hidden macaroons: correct.
- Nora's secret: borrowed money to fund the life-saving trip to Italy for Torvald's health; told him it came from her father; secretly repaying from housekeeping + copying work; forged her dying father's signature on the bond (the signature dated after his death): correct.
- Mrs Christine Linde (poor widow, old friend) seeks work; later revealed to have once loved Krogstad, left him to marry for money: correct.
- Nils Krogstad: the lender, now a clerk at Torvald's bank, himself disgraced by a past forgery, raising children; threatened with dismissal; blackmails Nora (keep his job or he exposes the forgery); the law indifferent to her motive: correct.
- Torvald's speech that a liar/forger poisons his home and infects his children (unknowingly describing Nora): correct.
- Dr Rank: family friend, dying of inherited disease (father's dissolution), secretly loves Nora, confesses: correct.
- Krogstad dismissed; returns wanting more; drops the exposing letter in the locked letterbox (Torvald has the key): correct.
- The tarantella: Nora begs Torvald to coach her dance to keep him from the letters; dances wildly ("as if your life depended on it"): correct.
- Mrs Linde & Krogstad reunite; she tells him to leave the letter so the truth comes out: correct.
- Torvald reads the letter; rages (hypocrite/criminal/unfit mother/her father's morals); decrees they keep up appearances but she loses the children — pure self-interest; no gratitude: correct.
- Krogstad's 2nd letter returns the bond & withdraws the threat; Torvald "I am saved!" then "you too"; instantly forgiving/condescending ("doubly my own", his child & wife): correct.
- The awakening: Nora changes clothes, the first serious talk in 8 years; passed from father to husband; doll-wife / playroom / children as dolls; "duties to myself" vs "wife and mother"; "before all else a reasonable human being"; stopped loving him when he thought only of himself; the "wonderful thing" (a true marriage of equals); returns the ring; leaves; the door slams: correct.
- The famous "door slam" ending, and Ibsen's forced alternative ("happy") ending which he called a "barbaric outrage" and disowned: correct.

## Judgment calls
- Ibsen's own resistance to the "feminist play" label (he framed it as about human freedom generally) is noted in the context scene for accuracy, without undercutting the play's clear force on women's position.
- The play is treated with genuine moral ambiguity in the close (Nora abandoning her children; brave vs selfish) rather than as a one-note triumph — faithful to how Ibsen actually leaves it.
- Translation note: as a translated play, "verbatim" means verbatim to the PD English translation (Gutenberg #2542), not to the Norwegian; stated implicitly by sourcing. Pet-name wording (songbird/squirrel) paraphrased in narration; the three carded lines are exact.
