Notes on the quotes:
- **quote-ambition** uses ellipses to trim one clause for pace. The narration speaks

  creature on the Mer de Glace, Vol II, Ch II). Using two from one chapter is a
  deliberate choice — that speech is the thematic heart of the book.

## Plot claims (checked against the source)

- Frame: Walton's Arctic expedition rescues Victor from the ice; Victor is pursuing
  "one who fled from me" (Letter IV, line 517). ✓
- Creation on "a dreary night of November"; Victor is revolted and flees (Vol I,
  Ch IV, lines 1425–1470). ✓
- William strangled; Justine wrongly executed. The planted evidence is **William's
  miniature portrait of Caroline** (Victor's mother), which the creature slips onto
  the sleeping Justine — William's miniature at line 1976; the creature takes "a
  portrait of a most lovely woman" at line 4447. ✓ (Earlier draft called this a
  "locket"; corrected to "miniature portrait" in the scene note.)
- Creature self-educates beside the De Lacey cottagers; is driven out; "I was
  benevolent and good; misery made me a fiend" (Vol II). ✓
- Demand for a mate; Victor agrees, then destroys the half-made female in the
  Orkneys as the creature watches (Vol III, Ch III). ✓
- "I shall be with you on your wedding-night" (Vol III, Ch III, lines 5342–5343). ✓
- Clerval strangled; Elizabeth murdered on the wedding night; Victor's father dies
  of grief; Victor pursues the creature into the Arctic (Vol III, Ch IV–VII). ✓
- Context scene: Mary Shelley began the novel at 18 during the 1816 "year without a
  summer" at the Villa Diodati ghost-story challenge; the Promethean subtitle; the
  galvanism/electricity backdrop. Standard biographical/contextual facts (not from
  the novel text); widely documented. ✓ (framing, not plot)

## Things I was unsure of / judgment calls for you

1. **Edition & chapter numbering.** Citations follow the **1818** three-volume
   edition (Vol I = 7 ch, Vol II = 9 ch, Vol III = 7 ch). The **1831** edition (which
   some exam boards set) renumbers all chapters into one sequence of 24, so a
   student working from 1831 would cite, e.g., the creation as Ch 5, not Vol I Ch IV.
   If your audience is mostly 1831-board students, say so and I'll add 1831
   equivalents in brackets on the quote cards. (Locked decision: 1818 — ARCH D11.)
2. **"Locket" vs "miniature portrait."** The text says a miniature portrait of
   Caroline, not a locket. Fixed in the note; narration never used "locket."
